Before conducting any analysis, I must first determine the geometry that I am going to use on my truss. The constraints of the project will be the biggest factor in designing the truss. I know that the supports, a pin and a roller, have two perpendicular reaction forces and once reaction force respectively. This means that there will be no moment reactions, therefore, I will need a member of my truss to hold the vertical load. Since I am constructing a truss, I know that I need to use only members in tension or compression, so a member spanning the distance from the supports with the loads suspended mid span is not an option. I will need to create a pinned connection above both of the loads to properly support them. This means using three horizontal members of equal length to span the distance between the supports. I will need another vertical force applied to the joint in order for it to be in equilibrium, so a vertical member above the joints is applied. Now for the joint above to be in equilibrium I will create a member spanning from the support to this joint, which will then support the vertical load from the applied force. Since this member is not on the vertical axis, there is also the horizontal force applied to the joint which will be balanced by a member spanning between the two upper joints, This will balance both upper joints and leave the truss as a whole in static equilibrium. The members with the highest force will therefore be the members spanning from the supports to the upper joints because they are resisting forces in both the vertical and horizontal.
